# Workshop: Requirements & Planning – Site Maps & Content Hierarchy

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this workshop, students will be able to:

* Identify and document the **content inventory** of a website project.
* Create a **site map** showing content hierarchy and navigation.
* Align the site structure with usability and accessibility principles.
* Prepare a planning deliverable for their development project.

---

## 2. Preparation & Materials

**Instructor prep:**

* Slides (brief on requirements gathering, content hierarchy, site maps).
* Examples of good vs. poor site maps (real-world screenshots).

**Student materials (templates provided):**

* **Content Inventory Template (spreadsheet or table):**
  \| Page/Section | Content Item | Type (Text, Image, Video, Form) | Source | Priority | Notes |
* **Sitemap Template (diagramming):**

  * Free online tools: [draw.io](https://app.diagrams.net/), [Miro](https://miro.com), [Lucidchart free tier](https://www.lucidchart.com), or even pen & paper.
  * Example shape library (rectangles = pages, arrows = navigation links).

---

## 3. Step-by-Step Tutorial + Workshop

### Step 1: Framing the Problem (20 min)

1. Short discussion: *“Why does planning matter before coding?”*

   * Avoids wasted effort, helps usability, supports accessibility.
   * Site map = blueprint; content inventory = materials list.
2. Instructor demo: Show a live site (e.g., college homepage). Walk through its top-level structure and navigation.

---

### Step 2: Select a Relevant Website Domain (10 min)

* Each student chooses a project scenario that matters to them (e.g., **personal portfolio, club site, small business, event page**).
* Write down in 2–3 sentences: *“What is this site for? Who is the main audience?”*
  *(This anchors their later decisions.)*

---

### Step 3: Content Inventory (30–40 min)

1. **Brainstorm content needs.** Ask:

   * What information must the site deliver?
   * What actions should users be able to take?
   * What media types are needed (text, images, forms)?
2. **Fill in template.** Students complete the **Content Inventory Table**.

   * Start with Homepage, About, Contact, then add pages/features relevant to their scenario.
   * Mark **priority** (High = essential, Medium = important, Low = optional/future).
3. **Peer check:** Swap tables with a peer, give quick feedback on missing content.

---

### Step 4: Build a Site Map (40 min)

1. **Intro to site map diagrams.** Instructor shows examples:

   * Good = clear hierarchy, 2–3 levels deep, logical grouping.
   * Poor = spaghetti, inconsistent labels, too many top-level items.
2. **Student activity:**

   * Use draw\.io, Miro, Lucidchart, or paper to diagram their site map.
   * Ensure pages from the Content Inventory appear in the hierarchy.
   * Organize logically (e.g., “About” under “Company,” “Team” under “About”).
3. **Checklist for a good sitemap:**

   * ≤ 7 top-level items (supports human memory).
   * Clear grouping of related content.
   * Consistent labeling (avoid “About Me” and “Who I Am” for the same thing).
   * Accessibility note: navigation order should match reading order.

---

### Step 5: Reflection & Deliverable (20 min)

* Students submit a **combined document** (content inventory + site map diagram + 1-paragraph rationale).
* Reflection prompts:

  * *How did your intended audience shape your sitemap?*
  * *What changes might you need to make later for accessibility?*
  * *Was anything hard to organize — why?*

---

## 4. Deliverables

* **Content Inventory Table** (completed template).
* **Site Map Diagram** (digital or photo of hand-drawn).
* **Short Rationale (150 words max).**

---

## 5. Assessment (Formative)

* Completeness of content inventory (all key pages covered).
* Logical site map structure, with clear hierarchy and navigation.
* Reflection shows awareness of audience and accessibility.
